ANNOTATION SCRIPT FOR tests/text/base.pdf
=========================================

Purpose
-------
One topic: the string recovered from ONE annotation's quads. Which
glyphs a quad claims, and which characters those glyphs become.

Output sequence is tests/order. Page attributes are tests/page. What
the producer wrote into the annotation dictionary is tests/inventory
and tests/comments. Dictionaries no producer will write are
tests/structure.

Everything here is on one page, in a single column, with generous
margins. No item in this family should ever fail because of where the
quad is; only because of what came out of it.

base.pdf contains no annotations. Annotate it by hand in each program
you want to test, following the list below, then save each result as

    base-<tool>-<version>.pdf        e.g. base-okular-26.04.3.pdf

into producers/. All of them are compared against ONE expectation set,
so any difference in the output is a difference in how that program
writes annotations.

Ground rules
------------
1. Do not let the tool flatten, optimise, or "print to PDF" the file.
2. Check the tool writes annotations INTO the PDF, not into a sidecar.
3. Sentinels are [[TXnn]]. ALWAYS include the sentinel in the selection.
   It is how a test knows which annotation it is looking at, and how it
   detects text bleeding in from a neighbouring line.
4. Type comments exactly as written. Copy-paste rather than retype.
5. Yellow unless told otherwise. Record tool name and version.

One addition specific to this family:

6. THE SENTINEL IS AT THE START, NOT THE END, IN TX3A AND TX3C. Those
   selections deliberately stop inside a word, so they cannot end with
   one. Read each item rather than working from the pattern.

If your tool cannot make a selection an item asks for, do not
approximate it. Leave the item out and add an entry to skips.json with
evidence, as tests/basic does for Okular and E8A.

Twelve items over twelve targets. TX2X and TX2Y are decoys and take no
annotation at all.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
TX1 -- ligature at a real word boundary
----------------------------------------------------------------------

"tariff" and "staff" end in a single ff ligature glyph, which this
document maps to U+FB00, the Unicode presentation form. A real space
follows it. A tool that rewrites U+FB0x with a pattern that also
swallows following whitespace closes that gap and joins the two words.

"offer" carries the same ff ligature but mid-word, with no space after
it, and "fixed" carries a decomposed fi. Together they are the control:
whatever TX1A and TX1B do, TX1C must be unaffected.

TX1A   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   "the marginal tariff [[TX1A]]"
       Comment:  Ligature ends the word; a real space follows.

TX1B   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   "a tariff on imported staff [[TX1B]]"
       Comment:  Two word-final ligatures in one selection.

TX1C   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   "the fixed offer [[TX1C]]"
       Comment:  Control: ligatures with no space after them.

       NOTE:     The five U+FB00 glyphs in this document are all in
                 TX1 and TX3C. If a later change to the preamble or the
                 font loses the presentation form, these items still
                 pass and stop testing anything:

                   pdftotext base.pdf - | grep -c $'\ufb00'   # 5

----------------------------------------------------------------------
TX2 -- adjacent lines, tight leading
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Four lines eight points apart, so each line's glyph boxes reach about
1.7pt into its neighbours'. Only the middle two are annotated. [[TX2X]]
above and [[TX2Y]] below are DECOYS: they must appear in no extracted
text anywhere. Leave them unannotated.

The block is deliberately cramped and awkward to select. That is the
point. Zoom in; take the time to get exactly one line.

TX2A   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   "the target line itself, selected on its own [[TX2A]]"
                 -- that line ONLY.
       Comment:  One line from a tight block.

TX2B   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   "the line below the target, also selected on its own
                 [[TX2B]]" -- that line ONLY.
       Comment:  The line directly below TX2A.

       NOTE:     After saving, check the quads in THIS producer's file
                 actually overlap the neighbouring lines. A tool that
                 writes quads tight to the glyph boxes does not
                 exercise the case at all, and its pass means nothing:

                   python3 -c "import pikepdf; \
                     print([list(a['/QuadPoints']) for a in \
                     pikepdf.open(FILE).pages[0]['/Annots']])"

                 Record what you find in PRODUCERS.md.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
TX3 -- quad edges inside a word
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Each selection stops or starts mid-word, so the quad edge falls between
two glyphs of one word and, in most producers, of one text-showing
operator. There are two opposite failure modes -- clipping the last
covered glyph away, and over-capturing to the end of the whole word or
span -- and both are measured here.

TX3C is different, and is the one item where the edge falls INSIDE a
glyph. A selection snaps to a character boundary, which is normally a
glyph boundary too; a ligature is one glyph carrying two characters, so
stopping between them is the only way to split a glyph by hand.

Most tools snap a drag-selection to word boundaries. Use a
character-level selection if the tool offers one.

TX3A   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   START at "[[TX3A]]" and END in the MIDDLE of
                 "premium", after the "u":
                   "[[TX3A]] a persistent leverage premiu"
       Comment:  Ends inside a glyph run.
       NOTE:     The sentinel is at the START of this one.

TX3B   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   START in the MIDDLE of "marginal", before the "g":
                   "ginal contribution [[TX3B]]"
       Comment:  Starts inside a glyph run.

TX3C   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   START at "[[TX3C]]" and END as close to the first 'f' as
                 possiblebetween the two f's of "payoff":
                   "[[TX3C]] the annual payo"
       Comment:  Edge inside a ligature glyph.
       NOTE:     The sentinel is at the START of this one.
       Expect:   the ligature EXCLUDED, so the recovered text ends
                 "payo". A glyph cannot be half-emitted, and the
                 canonical rule drops a glyph whose advance lies mostly
                 outside the quad. Emitting the whole "payoff" is
                 defensible; expected.json records the choice.

       NOTE:     TX3 is the section most at risk of being unproducible.
                 If NO available viewer offers character-level
                 selection, TX3A and TX3B cannot be hand-annotated and
                 move to tests/structure, where a quad edge can be
                 written directly. TX3C is likely to be unproducible
                 even where the others are not, because a tool may
                 refuse to place a caret inside a ligature. Record the
                 attempt in skips.json first: the escalation has to be
                 evidenced, not assumed.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
TX4 -- hyphenated line breaks
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Both blocks break at a hyphen, and nothing in the PDF distinguishes
them: a discretionary hyphen and a compound hyphen are the same glyph
in the same position. This is the ambiguity the README documents,
pinned here so that the default and --keep-hyphens each have a fixed
answer.

TX4A   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   the WHOLE block, "the surrounding infra-" through
                 "[[TX4A]]", in ONE selection across both lines.
       Comment:  Break inside one word.

TX4B   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   the WHOLE block, "a memory-" through "[[TX4B]]", in
                 ONE selection across both lines.
       Comment:  Break on a compound hyphen.

       NOTE:     AMBIGUITY, and a decision rather than a defect. Under
                 the default TX4A is right and TX4B is wrong; under
                 --keep-hyphens the reverse. No setting makes both
                 right, which is the point of the pair.

                 This corpus keeps no variant lists -- see the
                 comparison note in expected.json -- so following
                 tests/basic E2A, canonical assumes DEFAULT options.
                 TX4B's canonical value is therefore the tool's answer,
                 "memorymapped", and not what the document means. That
                 is the one place in this family where the two differ,
                 and expected.json says so in TX4B's own notes.

       NOTE:     TX5B contains "co-operative", a hyphen in the middle
                 of a line rather than at a break. It must survive
                 under BOTH settings. If it does not, the defect is in
                 hyphen handling generally, not in the line-break rule,
                 and TX4 will mislead you about where to look.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
TX5 -- non-ASCII in the covered text
----------------------------------------------------------------------

Accents, dashes and quotation marks in the DOCUMENT, decoded through
the font's /ToUnicode. This is a separate path from the comment
decoding in tests/comments and shares none of its code, which is why
it needs its own items rather than riding on CM1.

TX5A   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   the whole line:
                   the Größe of a café — naïve, précis, Ångström [[TX5A]]
       Comment:  Accents and an em dash in the covered text.
       Expect:   Größe, café, naïve, précis, Ångström, and U+2014 with
                 a space either side.

TX5B   Type:     Highlight
       Select:   the whole line, including the curly quotation marks:
                   the “efficient” frontier’s edge – a co-operative fixed point [[TX5B]]
       Comment:  Curly quotes, an apostrophe and an en dash.
       Expect:   U+201C and U+201D around efficient, U+2019 in
                 frontier's, U+2013 between edge and a, and a plain
                 ASCII hyphen in co-operative.

       NOTE:     The quotation marks in the PDF are curly, U+201C and
                 U+201D. If they come back as ASCII " then something in
                 the chain is normalising, which is a silent change to
                 the author's text and worth knowing about even if it
                 looks harmless.

       NOTE:     "efficient" carries a decomposed ffi, unlike TX1's ff.
                 A tool that passes TX1 and fails here is treating the
                 two ligature forms differently.
